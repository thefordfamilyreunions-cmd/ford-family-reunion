Our Roots

The story of James Hiram Ford & Jemima Anderson

Every branch of this family traces back to one beginning. Two people who built a life together in the Midwest — and whose descendants now stretch across generations, careers, and cities, many of whom have yet to find each other.

This is where we come from. This is who we are.

The Origin

James Hiram Ford
& Jemima Anderson

James Hiram Ford was born in Indiana in 1849, a son of William Ford and Priscilla Casey who married in 1841. Like so many Black families of that era, the Fords were part of the great Midwestern story — building lives and futures in a region that would shape generations to come.

In 1867, James married Jemima Anderson, born in Michigan in 1849 to Jefferson Anderson and Tabitha Madry. Jemima was described as mulatto — a woman whose own story carries the complexity and resilience of her time.

Together, James and Jemima built a family of eight children, rooted in Indiana and Michigan. Among them: Jasper Ford, whose family carries the reunion tradition forward today, and Maude Ford, whose line connects to the Rouse and Stewart families.

Their story is the trunk of our tree. Every reunion, every connection, every Ford family member alive today traces back to this union.

James Hiram Ford
& Jemima Anderson

Married 1867 · Indiana

James H. Ford — born Indiana, 1849. Son of William Ford & Priscilla Casey.
Jemima Anderson — born Michigan, 1849. Daughter of Jefferson Anderson & Tabitha Madry.
8 children — William Jefferson, Charlotte "Lottie", Asa, Ezekel "Zeke", Maude "Maudie", Ollie, Raymond, and Jasper.
Rooted in Indiana & Michigan — part of the great Black Midwestern story of the late 19th century.
Jemima Ford — died August 3, 1916 in Cassapolis, Michigan of cardiac complications.
James Hiram Ford — died August 5, 1919 in Cassapolis, Michigan of chronic cardio renal disease.
Their legacy — descendants now spread across the country, many meeting for the first time in Chicago 2027.
